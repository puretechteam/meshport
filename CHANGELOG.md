# Changelog

Newest first. Each entry is dated by when it landed.

## 2026-10-30: meshport 0.3.0, USD, Alembic, .blend, animation, skins and blend shapes

- **Added: USD**, read and written (`.usda` text, `.usdc` binary, `.usd`, and `.usdz` packages).
  - Reading composes the stage in USD's strength order: sublayers, inherits, variant selections (a stronger layer's choice wins over the asset's own), references and payloads (to other files, inside a package, or within the file), and specializes. Paths are mapped through each reference, and the stage can add to a class that a referenced asset inherits. An inherit loop is cut where USD cuts it. Instancing is included.
  - **Point instancers:** each instance becomes a node placed by the instancer's positions, orientations and scales, holding its prototype with the prototype's own transform. All instances share the prototype's meshes. Hidden (`invisibleIds`) and deactivated (`inactiveIds`) instances are left out, and the prototypes aren't drawn on their own. Then transforms of every kind, stage up axis and meters per unit (USD's default is centimeters), meshes with normals, UV sets and display colors in every interpolation, indexed primvars, holes and left-handed orientation, per-face materials from GeomSubsets, the Cube, Sphere, Cylinder, Cone, Capsule and Plane prims, and UsdPreviewSurface materials with their textures, followed through material and node-graph inputs. Invisible, inactive, guide, proxy and class-only prims are left out. Binary files from crate version 0.4 onward are read.
  - Writing produces Y-up, meter-based stages with a default prim, UsdPreviewSurface materials and textures (re-encoded to PNG when USDZ can't hold them); `.usdz` packages hold their textures, and `save()` writes them in a `textures` folder next to a `.usd`, `.usda` or `.usdc`. Binary layers use version 0.8.0, store repeated arrays once and compress index lists the way USD does (with real LZ4 when the `lz4` package is installed).
  - Checked against Pixar's own library: it reads meshport's files to the same geometry and its validators find no problems. meshport reads a 17-file corpus written by Pixar's library (`benchmarks/make_usd_corpus.py`) to the same geometry Pixar composes. The corpus includes inherits, implied inherits, specializes, an inherit loop and a point instancer, as text and binary. `benchmarks/compare_usd.py` and `tests/test_usd_interop.py` rerun this; `usd_points.py` expands point instancers with Pixar's own `ComputeInstanceTransformsAtTime`.
  - Binary files' integer list ops (such as `inactiveIds`) are read.
- **Added: Alembic** (`.abc`), read and written: transforms of every kind (and ones that ignore their parent), PolyMesh and SubD meshes with normals, UV sets and colors in every scope, indexed or not, and face sets as material slots. Alembic has no materials, so writing turns them into face sets named after them. Old HDF5 Alembic files are refused with a clear error.
- **Added: `.blend` files**, read only, from Blender 2.8 onward, including Blender 5's new file layout and zstd- or gzip-compressed files: mesh objects in the open scene with their hierarchy, every rotation mode, delta transforms and collection instances; all three ways Blender has stored meshes (and positions in a `position` layer next to the old polygon and loop arrays, though Blender 3.5 itself turned out to save the old arrays); UV maps, color attributes, per-face materials, flat and smooth shading; Principled BSDF materials with packed or external image textures. Modifiers, curves, text and metaballs: see below.
- **Added: `.blend` modifiers.** The modifiers that are on in Blender's viewport are applied in stack order (`blend.read(..., modifiers=False)` leaves them off). Their settings are read by name through the file's struct catalogue, so they work across versions. Others are skipped and the rest of the stack still applies.
  - Triangulate (every quad method; n-gons by ear clipping), Weld (All and Connected; merged points averaged, as Blender does), Mirror (any axes, merging to the seam's midpoint, bisecting, a mirror object, flipped and offset UVs), Array (fixed count or fit length; relative, constant and object offsets; merging, also of the last copy with the first; UV offsets) and Solidify (simple mode, even thickness, rims, flipped normals, rim only, material offsets) give Blender's points to float precision on a 23-object test file (`tests/data/modifiers.blend`, rebuilt by `make_modifier_fixture.py`), with the same triangle counts. Solidify's complex mode is approximated by simple mode with even thickness.
  - Subdivision Surface (Catmull-Clark or simple, at the viewport level, with both boundary modes) places points on the exact limit surface. That matches Blender to float precision up to Blender's Quality setting (3 by default); above it Blender approximates near points where other than four faces meet (0.3% of the size at level 6 on Blender's Sculpting template). UVs and colors are interpolated linearly; edge creases are ignored.
  - Not applied: Array caps and fit-to-curve, vertex groups, and other modifiers (Bevel, Boolean, Geometry Nodes, ...).
- **Added: curve, text and metaball objects in `.blend` files** become meshes, as Blender shows them (new `blendcurve.py`). Checked on a 25-object test file (`tests/data/curves.blend`, rebuilt by `make_curve_fixture.py`):
  - Curves: Bezier, poly and NURBS splines (uniform, endpoint and Bezier knots, or the knots stored in the file), each sampled at its resolution, with a round bevel, an extrusion or both, every fill mode, fill caps, per-point radius and tilt, and minimum-twist or Z-up frames. 2D curves are filled, with curves inside others cut out as holes. 20 of the 22 curve and text objects give Blender's points to float precision, with the same triangle counts. The other two are an open NURBS curve with an extrusion, whose frames turn about 3° differently, and text in Blender's built-in font (below). Modifiers on curves are applied too. Left out: bevel and taper objects, custom bevel profiles, the Tangent twist (treated as Minimum), bevel start and end factors, path offset, text on a curve, and NURBS surfaces.
  - Text: glyph outlines through the optional `fonttools` package (now in the `blend` extra), laid out as Blender does (font scale, character, word and line spacing, horizontal and vertical alignment, offsets, shear). Text in a font packed in the file (or found next to it) matches Blender to float precision, holes in letters included. Blender's built-in font isn't stored in files, so a similar installed font stands in, scaled to Blender's capital height; widths come out a few percent off. Text boxes, small caps, underlines and kerning are left out.
  - Metaballs: ball, capsule, plane, ellipsoid and cube elements, negative ones included, each adding stiffness · (1 - d²/r²)³ to the field, with every object of a family (`Mball`, `Mball.001`, ...) joined into the base object, as in Blender. The surface is found by marching tetrahedra at the viewport resolution, with each point moved onto the surface by bisection. Its points differ from Blender's, but measured against a fine reference it is as close to the true surface as Blender's own mesh.
- **Checked: `.blend` files from Blender 2.83, 2.93, 3.3 and 3.5**, each version's own files against its own GLB export: the test scene, the modifier and curve test files and the benchmark scenes. Everything agrees to float precision, apart from what meshport approximates in any version (above). Two new fixtures, `tests/data/scene_283.blend` (gzip) and `scene_35.blend` (zstd), keep this checked with every test run, UV maps and materials included. `tests/test_blend_corpus.py` (set `MESHPORT_BLEND_CORPUS`) now compares object by object with modifiers on, so a folder of all three test files from each version can be checked at once; the three fixture scripts take `-- OUT_DIR NAME` to write them.
  - **Fixed:** UV maps in files from Blender 2.8 to 3.4 (stored as `MLoopUV` structs) were read 16 bytes apart instead of the 12 the file says. Every mesh with a UV map from those versions was refused as damaged. The struct's size and layout now come from the file's own catalogue.
  - The fixture scripts work back to 2.83: settings an older Blender doesn't have are skipped (and printed), scale is set after adding a primitive, and the GLB export folds axis-angle rotations and delta transforms into plain ones (glTF exports before 3.0 drop them) and, before 2.93, turns curves and text into meshes (2.83's export leaves them out).
- **Added: animation, starting with glTF.** The scene model has `Scene.animations`: each `Animation` holds `Channel`s that move a node's translation, rotation or scale, with key times in seconds and glTF's three interpolations (step, linear, cubic spline). `channel.sample(t)` gives a value at any time (slerping linear rotations, held before the first key and after the last), `animation.local_matrix(node, t)` and `scene.walk_at(t)` pose the scene, and `animation.bake(rate)` resamples to evenly spaced linear keys, for formats that will need it.
  - glTF files are read and written with their animations. Animated nodes are written as translation, rotation and scale (glTF doesn't allow a matrix on them), and channels keyed at the same times share one accessor. A damaged channel (values that don't fit, a missing sampler or node, an accessor past its buffer) is left out instead of failing the whole file; keys out of order are sorted and keys with NaN times dropped. Morph-target weights aren't read yet.
  - Checked against Blender in both directions. Blender's exports (sampled per frame, and Bezier curves as cubic splines) come out posed as Blender poses them at every frame, to float precision. Blender's import of meshport's files lands on every key, though between keys it blends quaternion components where glTF slerps, and it builds its own curves for cubic splines.
  - **Fixed:** the glTF writer wrote `min`/`max` of a scalar accessor as a number instead of a one-item list, which Blender then refused as invalid. Only the new key-time accessors were scalar with bounds.
  - New helpers in `geometry.py`: `quaternion`, `matrix_quaternion`, `decompose_trs`, `compose_trs` and `slerp`.
- **Added: FBX animation**, read and written (new `fbxanim.py`).
  - Reading: every 7.x animation stack becomes an `Animation`. Each layer's curve nodes are followed to the models they move, and curves are evaluated in every kind FBX has: constant (also "constant next"), linear, and cubic (Bezier handles at each key's slopes, weighted or a third of the way along). FBX rotations are euler angles inside pivots, offsets and pre/post rotation, so each animated model is posed at all of its keys, and at every frame between where euler angles or cubic curves turn. Each pose is then split into translation, rotation and scale; models whose curves are all constant become STEP channels. Root models take the file's axis and unit conversion. Where several layers animate one property, the first layer's curves are used. A damaged curve is cleaned (keys sorted, NaN values dropped) or loses only its model's animation. FBX 6.x takes aren't read.
  - Writing: one animation stack and layer per animation, with a curve node per animated property and a curve per axis. Rotations are written as XYZ euler angles that turn continuously (each key takes whichever of its two equivalent forms, shifted by whole turns, is nearest the key before). Keys go at every frame of the frame rate the animation's keys sit on (24, 25, 30, 48, 50, 60, 100 or 120, else 30), which the file's TimeMode then records. STEP channels are held frame by frame too, since Blender's FBX import blends constant keys; translation and scale that are already linear keep their own keys.
  - **Checked against Autodesk's FBX SDK** through Unity (`tests/test_autodesk_fbx.py`, with `MESHPORT_UNITY`). The SDK's pose at every frame matches meshport's within 0.1 mm for probe files with cubic keys (with their own slopes and with weighted handles), both kinds of constant key, and models with offsets, pivots, pre/post rotation and ZXY order. The SDK also poses meshport's own animated files as meshport does. Against Blender: Blender's FBX exports (with a key per frame) come out posed as Blender poses them, to float precision, and Blender's import of meshport's files lands on every frame. Blender's import puts the file's time 0 at frame 1. 100 animated nodes with 5,000 keys each write in 1.9 s and read in 1.1 s.
  - **Fixed:** the FBX SDK ignored meshport's animation entirely, because the animation layer had no list of child records (it only takes one that does, even when empty). Found by the new Unity check.
  - **Fixed (FBX reading, animated or not):** pre and post rotation are now applied only when the model's RotationActive is on, which is off by default. Autodesk's FBX SDK and Blender both work that way; meshport used to apply them always. The rotation order counts either way, as in the SDK (Blender drops it as well).
- **Added: USD, Alembic, 3DS and .blend animation** (node translation, rotation and scale).
  - **USD**, read and written. Reading takes every xform op's time samples, interpolated as USD does (held outside them, linear between, quaternions slerped), poses each animated prim at every sample time (and every time code between, unless only translations move), and splits the poses into channels; seconds are time codes over the root layer's timeCodesPerSecond. Layer offsets on sublayers, references and payloads (both codecs now read them; they used to be skipped) and each layer's own timeCodesPerSecond retime samples on the way up, as USD composes them. Writing gives each animated node translate, orient and scale ops with time samples, and the root layer timeCodesPerSecond, framesPerSecond and start and end time codes; both the text and binary codecs can now write time samples. USD has one time line, so the first animation is written.
  - **Alembic**, read and written. Property headers' sample counts, last changed samples and time-sampling indices are read now, and the archive's time samplings (uniform, cyclic and acyclic) give each Xform sample's time. Writing puts a transform sample every frame (of the rate the keys sit on) on one uniform time sampling.
  - **3DS**, read: the keyframer's position, rotation and scale tracks, every key now (only the first was read before), at 30 frames a second. Each rotation key turns on from the one before, composed after it, and keys blend in a straight line. Two real 3ds Max files saved part way through their animation settle this: posed on their saved frame, RotatingCube (182 rotation keys) and TargetCameraAnim (a camera between position keys) match the geometry 3ds Max stored, to float precision; reading rotation keys as absolute (as Blender's 3DS importer does) or blending keys as TCB splines misses by far. Spline terms on keys are read past. `tests/test_corpus_3ds.py` now checks every file saved part way through this way.
  - **.blend**, read (new `blendanim.py`): each object's action, as Blender 2.8 to 4.3 keep it (a list of F-curves) and as 4.4 and later do (layered actions: layers, strips, channel bags per slot). Keys are evaluated as Blender evaluates them: Bezier (handles shortened together when they overlap, the curve solved for the frame), linear, constant, and every easing mode by Robert Penner's published equations, with constant or linear extrapolation. They drive location, every rotation mode, scale and the delta transforms, through the parent inverse matrix, at the scene's frame rate. On a 49-object test file from Blender 5.1 and 2.83 (`tests/data/anim.blend`, `anim_283.blend`, rebuilt by `make_anim_fixture.py`), every object is where Blender puts it at every frame checked, except where Blender changes Penner's equations: exponential easing (by up to 0.1% of the change) and elastic easing (whose wobble Blender scales by the key's amplitude). F-curve modifiers, drivers and the NLA are left out.
  - Checked against Blender both ways for USD and Alembic (Blender's exports posed as Blender poses them, at every frame, and Blender's imports of meshport's files likewise), and against Pixar's own library (0.25, the copy inside Blender): it poses meshport's animated .usda, .usdc and .usdz as meshport does, its compliance checker finds nothing, and it times layered files with sublayer and reference offsets and different timeCodesPerSecond the same as meshport.
  - `geometry.py` gains the batched `euler_many`, `decompose_many` and `rotations` (moved from `fbxanim.py`), and `scene.py` `frame_rate`.
- **Added: skins and blend shapes** (glTF's skins and morph targets), read from glTF, FBX, USD and .blend and written to glTF, FBX and USD.
  - **Scene model.** `Skin` (joints, inverse bind matrices, per-vertex joint indices and weights) on `Node.skin`; `Morph` (position and normal offsets) in `Mesh.targets`, with `Mesh.target_weights` and `Mesh.morphed()`; a `"weights"` channel path, `Animation.weights()`, `Scene.posed_points()` (blend shapes, then the skin) and `Scene.deformed()` (both applied where they stand, for formats without them). As in glTF, a skinned mesh's own node plays no part in where its vertices go.
  - **glTF**: skins (every JOINTS_n/WEIGHTS_n set), morph targets with normals, target names (`extras.targetNames`), default weights on the mesh or node, and weights channels in all three interpolations. Damaged skins and targets are dropped, not the model.
  - **FBX**: Skin deformers and clusters (the cluster's Transform is the inverse bind matrix; Total1 clusters leave the rest of each vertex with the mesh's node, as Autodesk's SDK does), BlendShape deformers and DeformPercent curves. Writing adds LimbNode skeleton models, a bind pose and sparse shapes.
  - **USD**: UsdSkel read and written: Skeletons, SkelAnimations, joint primvars, geomBindTransform, BlendShapes and their weights matched by name. The binary codec can now write quaternion arrays and array-valued time samples, and the text codec writes `matrix4d[]` as Pixar's parser wants it (it used to write them flat).
  - **.blend** (new `blendskin.py`): armatures (bones as nodes, pose bone curves), the armature modifier's vertex groups (on the mesh from 3.0, on the object before; in a vertex data layer in 5.x), and relative shape keys with their vertex groups and their own action.
  - **Alembic, 3DS and OBJ** get each mesh bent where it stands.
- **Added: in-between shapes** in FBX (several shapes on one channel, at their FullWeights) and USD (`inbetweens:NAME` at their weight). Each shape becomes a target of its own, driven by the channel so the blend is exactly FBX's and UsdSkel's (linear between neighbouring shapes, the ends carried on), with a weight key wherever the channel passes a shape. Autodesk's FBX SDK (through Unity) and Pixar's UsdSkel blend the test files exactly as meshport does.
- **Added: Alembic keeps skinned and blend-shape motion.** A mesh its skin or blend shapes bend over time is written with its points every frame instead of standing still; Blender plays the file as meshport poses the scene. Reading, a point cache (points that change, faces that don't) comes in as a blend shape per sample, weighted so they play back as Alembic plays them, up to 30 million values (`alembic.CACHE_BUDGET`).
- **Added: Blender's bone inheritance options** in .blend files: inherit rotation off, and inherit scale Full, Average, None and None (Legacy), worked out from files Blender saves (`tests/data/make_bone_fixture.py`; `bones.blend` and `bones_283.blend` from Blender 5.1 and 2.83) and exact against Blender's poses. Inherit rotation off with Full scale, Fix Shear and Aligned are exact while the parent scales evenly; under uneven scaling their poses shear, which animation channels can't hold, and come out close (within 0.6 of a unit on the test rig, at its most uneven).
- **Fixed: rigs whose skinned mesh hangs under a moved node** (CesiumMan, BrainStem, RiggedFigure, RiggedSimple) came into Blender wrongly from meshport's FBX (off by up to 87% of the model's size) and USD (up to 120%). The FBX writer now places a skinned mesh where its joints bound it, writes the armature's bind matrix (TransformAssociateModel, which Blender places the mesh by) and puts the armature in the bind pose; the USD writer makes bindTransforms the joints at rest and geomBindTransform the bind, and writes such a mesh under the SkelRoot (Blender takes geomBindTransform as relative to the mesh's parent). Now all eight animated Khronos samples agree with Blender both ways to 1e-5 of their size.
- **Fixed: some files couldn't be written as USD** (an OverflowError, Khronos' BrainStem for one): the binary integer tables now wrap 32-bit steps as USD does.
- **Fixed: GLB validator errors** after a trip through other formats: inverse bind matrices could end in 1e-17 instead of an exact 0 (ACCESSOR_INVALID_IBM), and a key 1e-7 s from a frame sample could make glTF's 32-bit times stand still (3192 errors on BrainStem through FBX). Skinned meshes are written as roots without a transform, as glTF recommends.
- **Tested against real rigs and every reference** (`tests/test_real_rigs.py`): Khronos' nine sample models through Blender (both ways), Pixar's UsdSkel, Autodesk's FBX SDK and Khronos' glTF Validator; and Autodesk's SDK now bends meshport's skinned FBX too (`tests/test_autodesk_fbx.py`), within 1e-4 at every frame (Unity normalizes cluster weights as it imports, so it can't check Total1).
  - **Checked against Blender and Pixar.** A skinned, shape-keyed tube (`tests/data/make_skin_fixture.py`; `skin.blend` and `skin_283.blend` saved by Blender 5.1 and 2.83) is posed by meshport as Blender poses it, to 1e-6 at every frame, from Blender's glTF, FBX, USD and .blend; Blender imports meshport's glTF, FBX and USD to the same points; Pixar's UsdSkel.BakeSkinning (the copy inside Blender) bakes meshport's USD skins and blend shapes to meshport's points, and its compliance checker finds nothing. Found on the way: the FBX cluster's Transform in the file is already the mesh-to-bone matrix (ufbx reads it the same way); Blender's glTF import plays only the first animation, so a skin's bones and its weights need to be in one.
- **Added:** `benchmarks/compare_blender.py --surface` compares surfaces (the farthest either surface is from the other) instead of points, for geometry the two build with different points. `--apply-modifiers` now applies modifiers on both sides.
- **Added:** `benchmarks/compare_blender.py` converts files to GLB with Blender and with meshport and compares speed and geometry: 19 of 19 of Blender's files agree, and meshport is 6 to 7 times faster.
- **Changed:** `meshport.FORMATS` lists the new formats, and content sniffing recognizes them.

- **Fixed (security): hostile files can't hang meshport, fill the memory or reach outside the model's folder.** A review of the new readers found files that would. Each case is now a test that builds the file in code (`tests/test_hostile.py`: the 54 tests that check a fix failed before it, and 10 more check that what should still load does), and `tests/test_fuzz.py` reads 1,400 fuzzed and 2,000 cut-short USD, Alembic and .blend files, which may only raise `MeshportError`. Limits sit far above what real models need; reaching one raises `MeshportError` with a plain message.
  - **Linked files** (USD sublayers, references and payloads, texture paths in FBX, 3DS, USD and .blend files, and font paths in .blend files) are read only from the model's own folder and the folder above it, and below them. Absolute paths, network paths, `file:` URLs and paths reaching further out are never opened; for textures and fonts, the file name is still looked for in those folders, and for fonts also in the system's font folders, so a font from the machine the file was made on is still found. A chain of USD references can't walk outward either: every file stays inside the top file's area. FBX and 3DS no longer look for textures in the working directory (when reading bytes without a folder), nor in texture folders two levels up.
  - **FBX:** a record ending before it starts made reading loop forever. Curves with huge attribute counts no longer allocate their product (100,000 keys took 32 s and 80 GB; now instant).
  - **USD binary:** reads past the end of the file fail instead of reading zeros, and counts are checked against the file (a variant map claiming 2^62 entries looped for good; a path table claiming 20 million paths allocated them). A value many specs share is decoded once, and decoding stops at 256 MB plus 16 times the file's size (40 arrays from 200 KB would have been 640 MB). Inlined `uchar` values keep only their byte.
  - **USD binary writing:** a layer whose prims don't list their children and properties wrote path names missing from the token table, so the file couldn't be read back. meshport's own scenes always list them, so their files are unchanged.
  - **LZ4:** checked with python-lz4 installed and without: both decoders agree on exact and upper-bound sizes, files written with python-lz4 are really compressed (under a quarter of the size) and read back either way, and data over 2 GB is written in chunks, as USD's own TfFastCompression does.
  - **USD composition:** a layer listed many times in a layer stack is used once (a stack listing the next layer 20 times, six deep, made 3.4 million layers); an arc that leads back to an opinion already on its way is dropped, as USD drops it (a reference loop with two arcs a step doubled at every step); a stage composes at most 4 million opinions (`usd.MAX_SITES`); point instancer copies count toward `scene.MAX_NODES`. USDZ packages must store their files uncompressed (as USDZ requires) with sizes that match, each file is read out once, and a package reads out at most 8 times its size plus 64 MB.
  - **.blend:** compressed files are inflated piece by piece, up to 4 GB (`blend.MAX_INFLATED`) and 256 times the file's size (at least 1 GB). Telling what a file is inflates only its first bytes, so gzip-compressed .blend files over 1 MB load again (they failed). Collection instances count toward `scene.MAX_NODES`; layer and attribute counts are clamped to their block; vertex weights and shape keys shared by many vertices or keys are read once, within 64 million weights and 100 million shape key values a mesh (`blendskin.WEIGHT_BUDGET`, `SHAPE_BUDGET`); modifiers and curves make at most 100 million corners a file (`blendmods.MAX_GENERATED`).
  - **Alembic:** only the samples a property stores are read (a header claiming 50 million samples looped for hours); point caches count their weights toward `alembic.CACHE_BUDGET` (6,000 samples made 288 MB of weights); an object group shown in many places is read once, with one shared mesh; objects count toward `scene.MAX_NODES`.
  - **New limits in `scene.py`:** `MAX_NODES` (1 million nodes a file, instances and copies included) and `MAX_FRAMES` (100,000 frames on an animation's frame grid, for FBX, USD, Alembic and .blend). Past the frame limit, readers keep the keys (FBX, USD) or leave out that animation (Alembic, .blend), and writers raise `MeshportError`.
  - **Robustness:** the USD, USD text and Alembic writers and `Scene.walk`, `walk_at` and `deformed` use loops instead of recursion, so any depth of nesting works (5,000 levels raised `RecursionError`). glTF accessors may hold 268 million values plus 4 per byte of the file's buffers (`gltf.MAX_ACCESSOR_VALUES`). A glTF node listed under several parents (which glTF forbids) is still placed under each, but the placements are counted first and count toward `scene.MAX_NODES` (a chain of 40 such nodes meant 2^40 copies and never ended; now refused in 0.1 ms). Ear clipping takes concave faces of up to 1,000 corners and 10 million steps a mesh (`geometry.EAR_CLIP_MAX`, `EAR_CLIP_BUDGET`); bigger ones are fanned (one face of 3,000 corners took 7.1 s, and the time grows with the square of its size).
- **Faster:** USD prims with many children or properties: a two-layer stage with 20,000 of each reads in 0.73 s instead of 5.22 s. List edits with 40,000 items and 20,000 deletions: 0.001 s instead of 2.51 s.
- **Docs:** the README's USD corpus is 17 files (it said 13), the install size is 610 KB for the whole package, the scene model lists skins and blend shapes, and LICENSE notes OpenUSD (Apache 2.0) and Alembic (BSD 3-Clause), whose formats meshport was written from.

## 2026-10-26: The FBX writer can't overflow the stack (after the 0.2.0 tag)

- **Fixed:** writing an FBX file nested a few thousand levels deep crashed Python 3.10 on Windows with a stack overflow. The writer now uses a loop instead of recursion, so any depth works. Its output is byte for byte the same. Found by the new GitHub Actions run on Python 3.10.

## 2026-10-26: Texture paths can't reach other files (part of 0.2.0)

- **Fixed (security):** a model's texture paths could name any file on the computer, and meshport would copy it into the FBX, 3DS or OBJ it wrote. A hostile model from the internet could use this to slip private files into a converted copy that then gets shared. Now:
  - only files that really are images (PNG, JPEG, TGA, BMP, TIFF, DDS, PSD, GIF, WebP, EXR, HDR, KTX, AVIF/HEIF, SGI) are taken, and only the first 64 bytes of anything else are read;
  - network paths (`\\server\share\...`) and URLs are never opened. On Windows, even checking whether one exists sends the user's login to that server;
  - glTF files may only point to paths relative to themselves, and their buffers must sit in the model's own folder.
- **Added:** tests for all of the above (`tests/test_damaged.py`).
- **Changed:** the README's install lines use the GitHub URL, since meshport isn't on PyPI yet.
- **Added:** GitHub Actions run the tests (Python 3.10 and 3.13 on Windows) and `ruff check` on every push and pull request, plus `CONTRIBUTING.md` and `SECURITY.md`. `ruff` is in the `dev` extra.

## 2026-10-25: meshport 0.2.0, Autodesk 3DS

- **Added:** reading and writing `.3ds` files (`meshport.load`/`save`, `meshport.read`/`write` with `"3ds"`, and the command line).
  - Reads mesh objects with their UVs, smoothing groups (turned into normals), per-face materials and object matrices, and materials with their diffuse color, opacity, shininess, two-sided flag, self-illumination, and texture, bump and self-illumination maps. Textures are found next to the model the same way as for FBX.
  - Writes every mesh as an object in world space, with an identity matrix. Meshes over the format's 65,535-vertex or 65,535-face limit are split into several objects. Hard edges are kept by splitting vertices under one smoothing group.
  - Names are cut to the format's limits (10 characters for objects, 16 for materials, 8.3 for texture files) and kept unique. `save()` writes the texture files next to the `.3ds`.
  - Reads the keyframer section for the hierarchy, pivots, dummy groups, instances and objects parented to cameras. Animated files open at their first frame, as in Blender and Assimp.
  - Skipped when reading: the animation itself, lights and cameras.
- **Added:** 3DS tests: round trips, smoothing groups, splitting, name limits, damaged and hostile files, and Blender interop in both directions. Blender's 3DS add-on reads meshport's files with the same vertex and face counts and every vertex in place, and meshport reads Blender's 3DS files (keyframer section included) to the same geometry and smoothing as Blender's own glTF export. These skip when Blender or its `autodesk_3ds_format` extension isn't installed.
- **Tested** against Autodesk's own 3DS reader (the FBX SDK, run through Unity): meshport's files, keyframer cases, and Assimp's 15 real-world `.3ds` sample files with meshport's re-saved copies all import with the same points, normals and UVs. The one exception is an object hung from a camera: Autodesk's reader ignores the camera's aim, while the file's stored geometry shows 3ds Max applies it, as meshport does. `tests/test_autodesk_3ds.py` (with `MESHPORT_UNITY`) and `tests/test_corpus_3ds.py` (with `MESHPORT_3DS_CORPUS`) rerun these checks.
- **Fixed while testing against Autodesk's reader and real files:**
  - The writer stored each object's matrix, and Autodesk's reader then drew every moved object at the origin. Objects are now written in world space with identity matrices, which Autodesk's reader and Blender both place correctly.
  - Smoothing-group normals count every face equally and are computed where the points are stored, exactly as Autodesk's reader does (this matters under non-uniform scale).
  - A material whose texture file is missing keeps its own color instead of turning white.
  - The writer no longer merges vertices that the mesh keeps apart at the same spot. Merging them turned zero-area faces into faces other programs delete.
  - A file saved on its first frame takes object placement from its stored geometry, which is exact, and still takes the hierarchy from the keyframer.
- **Changed:** the FBX reader's texture search is now shared with the 3DS reader.
- **Changed:** the code passes `ruff check` with ruff 0.16's wider default rules. No behavior changes.

## 2026-10-25: Sturdier against damaged files

- **Fixed:** damaged or deliberately malformed files now always raise `MeshportError`. Before, a corrupted GLB or glTF could raise a raw `UnicodeDecodeError`, `KeyError` or `IndexError`, and a deeply nested FBX could hit a `RecursionError`. Found by feeding meshport 1,600 corrupted files.
- **Fixed:** compressed FBX arrays are never inflated beyond the size the file declares, so a tiny file can't claim gigabytes of memory (a "zip bomb").
- **Added:** fuzz, zip-bomb and deep-nesting tests (`tests/test_damaged.py`).
- **Changed:** removed an unused import found by linting.

## 2026-10-25: Comparisons you can check

- **Added:** a "Why meshport?" section in the README. It compares meshport with Blender, ufbx, Assimp and the Autodesk FBX SDK on 2,079 real FBX files: size, what each can read and write, crashes, correctness and speed. It also lists where the others are ahead.

## 2026-10-25: OBJ export

- **Added:** writing Wavefront OBJ with its `.mtl` file and texture files.
  - Polygons stay polygons.
  - Each node becomes its own object in world space.
  - Mirrored nodes keep their faces pointing outward.
  - `meshport.save()` returns every file it wrote.
- **Why:** converting a 7.8 MB, 821-part FBX scene to OBJ took 5.9 s through trimesh, and 1.5 s with meshport writing it directly.

## 2026-10-25: meshport 0.1.0, first release

- **Reads FBX:** binary and ASCII, versions 6.x and 7.x (2006 onward), in either byte order.
- **Writes FBX:** binary 7.4, the version every importer accepts, with textures embedded.
- **Reads and writes glTF 2.0:** `.glb` and self-contained `.gltf`. Reading handles interleaved buffers, sparse accessors, triangle strips and fans, and external or embedded buffers. Draco and meshopt files get a clear error.
- **Carries across:**
  - the node hierarchy, with full FBX transforms (pivots, offsets, pre/post rotation, all six rotation orders, geometric transforms);
  - axis and unit conversion (Y-up, Z-up, cm, m, inches);
  - polygons, with concave faces triangulated correctly;
  - normals, every UV set, vertex colors;
  - instancing;
  - per-face materials with base color, opacity, emissive, roughness, and base color, normal and emissive textures.
- **Finds textures** that FBX files point at on the original artist's drive, by looking next to the model and in `Textures` folders up to two levels above it.
- **Command line:** `meshport in.fbx out.glb`, or `meshport file.fbx` to list what's inside.
- **Tested** against 2,079 real-world FBX files, against Autodesk's FBX SDK (through Unity) and against Blender's importer.
- **Not yet:** animation, skinning, blend shapes, cameras and lights.
